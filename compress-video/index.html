Compress a video in your browser

Free, private, in-browser video compression — nothing leaves your device. Choose a quality level, or a size target that fits Discord (20 MB) or an email attachment, and get a smaller MP4 made on your own device.

How video compression works here

This tool re-encodes your clip at a smaller size using ffmpeg, the standard video engine, compiled to run inside your browser. Your video is processed on your own device and never uploaded. Because video is heavy work, larger files take longer and use more memory — so it runs best on a computer rather than a phone.

When to compress a video

  • Email & upload limits: get a clip under the size caps on mail and web forms.
  • Faster sharing: send long recordings without a huge upload.
  • Saving storage: shrink videos before backing them up.
  • Posting online: meet the file-size limits of a platform or CMS.

Balancing size and quality

Lower quality settings produce smaller files with some visible loss; higher settings stay sharp but save less. If you only need part of the clip, trim it first — cutting length is the biggest size saving of all. MP4 (H.264) output is the most compatible choice for sharing.

Frequently asked questions

Is my video uploaded anywhere?

No. Compression runs entirely in your browser, so your video never leaves your device.

Will compressing reduce quality?

Some, depending on the setting. Start moderate and check the result before downloading.

How large a video can I compress?

The maximum is 350 MB per video; short clips under about 100 MB work best. Available browser memory may limit processing below the maximum, especially for high-resolution video. A computer is recommended.

Why does it take a while?

Video encoding is intensive and runs on your own device, so longer clips take longer. Nothing is waiting on a server, though.

What format is the output?

MP4 with H.264, the most widely compatible video format.
