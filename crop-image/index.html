Crop an image in your browser

Free, private, in-browser image cropping — nothing leaves your device. Pick a shape (free, square, 4:5, 16:9, 9:16 or 4:3), drag across the preview to choose what to keep, then download the cropped image.

How cropping works here

Drag a selection box over the part you want to keep, or choose a fixed aspect ratio, and the tool outputs just that region as a new image. The crop keeps the original pixels inside the selection, so there's no quality loss, and nothing is uploaded — it all happens on your device.

When to crop an image

  • Fit a shape: make a square avatar, a wide banner, or a portrait post.
  • Remove clutter: trim distracting edges and tighten the framing.
  • Focus attention: zoom in on the subject that matters.
  • Platform sizes: match the dimensions a social network or store expects.

Common aspect ratios

A few ratios cover most needs: 1:1 (square) for avatars and Instagram, 16:9 for video thumbnails and slides, 4:5 for portrait social posts, and 3:2 for standard photos. Cropping to a set ratio up front saves you from a platform re-cropping your image in a way you didn't intend. If you're unsure which to use, a square 1:1 crop is the safest all-purpose choice, since almost every platform and profile accepts it without further adjustment.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Cropping happens entirely in your browser, so your images never leave your device.

Does cropping lower the resolution?

No. Cropping keeps the original pixels within the selected area — you simply end up with a smaller region at full quality.

Can I crop to exact dimensions?

Yes. You can lock the crop to a shape such as square, 4:5 or 16:9. For exact pixel sizes, crop first and then use Resize image.

Which formats are supported?

JPG, PNG, WebP and iPhone HEIC photos.

Is there a size limit?

The maximum is 150 MB per image; under about 40 MB is recommended. High-resolution images may exceed available browser memory even below the maximum.
