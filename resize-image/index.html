Resize an image without uploading it

Free, private, in-browser image resizing — nothing leaves your device. Pick an exact size for Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, Etsy or Shopify, set your own width and height, or scale by percentage. Do one image or a batch, with results bundled into a ZIP.

How resizing works here

Enter a new width and height — or a percentage — and your browser redraws the image at that size, then gives you the result to download. It all runs locally, so nothing is uploaded. Lock the aspect ratio to keep proportions correct, or set exact dimensions when a form or platform demands a specific pixel size.

When to resize an image

  • Exact dimensions: hit the pixel sizes required for avatars, banners, or ID-photo uploads.
  • Smaller files: scale a large photo down before emailing it or posting it to the web.
  • Thumbnails: create small preview versions of larger images.
  • Fit a layout: size an image to match a slide, document, or print dimension.

Keeping images sharp when you resize

Scaling an image down almost always looks clean. Scaling up can't invent detail that isn't there, so enlarging a small image will look soft. Keep the aspect ratio locked to avoid stretching, and if you need the smallest possible file, resize first and then run it through the compressor.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Resizing happens entirely in your browser, so your images never leave your device.

Will resizing distort my image?

Not unless you ask it to. When the new size is a different shape, it crops to fill from the centre or fits the image inside a border. Only “Stretch to fit” changes the proportions.

Can I make an image larger?

Yes, but upscaling can't add real detail, so enlarged images tend to look softer than the original.

Can I resize a batch at once?

Yes. Add multiple images and download the resized set together as a ZIP.

Is there a size limit?

The maximum is 150 MB per image; under about 40 MB is recommended. Images run one at a time, but large batches and the final ZIP still need browser memory.
